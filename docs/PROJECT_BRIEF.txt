5. Reproduce the known spectral dimension before doing anything new

This is Gate A.

On the dual tetrahedron graph, each tetrahedron is a random-walk state.

Use the same lazy diffusion equation as the 3D spectral-dimension analysis:

$$ K(s,s_0,\sigma+1) = (1-\rho)K(s,s_0,\sigma) + \frac{\rho}{4} \sum_{s'\sim s}K(s',s_0,\sigma). $$

The published 3D analysis defines

$$ P(\sigma) = \frac{1}{N_3} \sum_s K(s,s,\sigma) $$

and

$$ D_s(\sigma) = -2\frac{d\log P}{d\log\sigma}. $$

For unit finite differences it used

$$ \boxed{ D_s(\sigma) = -\frac{\sigma}{P(\sigma)} \left[ P(\sigma+1)-P(\sigma-1) \right]. } $$

Implement two independent estimators

Estimator A — random walkers

For many starting tetrahedra:

stay with probability \(1-\rho\);
otherwise choose one of four neighbours uniformly;
count returns after every \(\sigma\).

Estimator B — stochastic trace

Construct

$$ M=(1-\rho)I+\frac{\rho}{4}A, $$

where \(A\) is dual adjacency.

Then

$$ P(\sigma)=\frac{1}{N_3}\operatorname{Tr}M^\sigma. $$

Use Hutchinson/Rademacher trace estimates:

$$ \operatorname{Tr}M^\sigma \approx \frac1R \sum_{r=1}^{R} z_r^TM^\sigma z_r. $$

This may massively reduce computation.

Validate estimator B against:

exact matrices on tiny triangulations;
random-walker estimator on medium triangulations.

Do not use it in production until the two agree within uncertainty.

10. The main methodological research task: 3D coarse-graining

This is Gate C and probably the hardest part.

Do not implement the naive graph nerve only

Why?

If two Voronoi cells meet along multiple disconnected interface components, collapsing all of them to one abstract edge destroys topology.

The 2D construction explicitly keeps different boundary segments between the same cells distinct; such multiplicity is involved in pinching behaviour.

Therefore Codex should construct an incidence-preserving dual cell complex.

13. Convert the dual cellulation into a genuine simplicial complex

20. Implement conditioned stochastic-trace estimation

Let

$$ \Pi_C $$

$$ P_C(\sigma) = \frac{1}{|C|} \operatorname{Tr} \left( \Pi_C M^\sigma \right). $$

$$ P_C(\sigma) \approx \frac{1}{R|C|} \sum_r z_r^TM^\sigma z_r. $$

$$ P_R(\sigma), \quad P_Q(\sigma), \quad P_{S_0}(\sigma), \quad P_{S_1}(\sigma), \quad P_{S_2}(\sigma) $$
